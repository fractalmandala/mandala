## Sanskrit Grammar and Dhātus

The [Sanskrit](/writings/blog/sanskrit) language notes document Amrit's systematic study of Pāṇinian grammar:

- **Dhātu Vikaraṇa** — the seven vikaraṇas (modifications) that mark the present tense: śap, śyan, śru, śa, śnam, u, śnā
- **Pada Classification** — subanta (nominal inflection) and tiṅanta (verbal inflection) systems
- **The Seven Primitive Bhāvas** — foundational categories of verbal meaning
- [Under Pāṇini's Light](/writings/blog/underpaninislight) - reading Indian history through Pāṇini's grammatical framework

The [Dhātuverse](/writings/blog/dhatuverse) essay (also counted under the dharmabook) is central here — showing how Pāṇinian dhātus reconnect us to civilizational consciousness through ontological onomatopoeia, the semantic consonance between sound and meaning. The study of vyākaraṇa (Sanskrit grammar) is itself a Vedāntika discipline capable of leading to yoga with Śabda Brahman.
